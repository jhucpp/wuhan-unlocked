WUHAN

isn't on your radar yet. It should be.

Two rivers crash into each other here, 1.3 million college students take over the streets every night, and breakfast is a bowl of noodles engineered to be eaten in under four minutes. This is the case for spending Thanksgiving break in the city that taught China how to build a bridge across the Yangtze — and never really slowed down since.

Get your bearings

Three cities, stitched together by bridges

Wuhan isn't really one city — it's Wuchang, Hankou, and Hanyang, three separate towns split apart by the Yangtze and Han rivers and welded back together by bridges (starting with the one that finally connected them in 1957). Before you go

The practical stuff

Best time

Late November is a genuinely great call: crisp, dry, jacket weather, red maples at their peak, and none of the spring cherry-blossom crowds. (Summer, by contrast, is brutally hot and humid — hard pass.)

Getting there

Direct and connecting flights into Wuhan Tianhe Airport, or a ~4–5 hour bullet train from Beijing, Shanghai, or Guangzhou.

Budget

Street food meals run $1–3. Overall, Wuhan is noticeably cheaper than Beijing, Shanghai, or Shenzhen — use the planner above for a real per-trip estimate.

One rule

Most places are app-pay only (Alipay/WeChat Pay) — set one up before you land, and keep your passport on you for hotel check-ins and museum reservations.

Wuhan doesn't do subtle. Neither should your itinerary.
